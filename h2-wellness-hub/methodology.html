Methodology / source and disclosure rules

Useful navigation needs visible boundaries.

H2 Wellness Hub uses Hydrogen Wellness Industry for product, channel, quality, and market content. Healthcare is reserved for hospital, clinical-research, and regulatory contexts. We separate research, regulation, testing, and commercial information so readers can see what a source does—and does not—establish.

Evidence

Research is mapped, not converted into a sales claim.

We show the source trail, study context, and stated limitations. We do not diagnose, treat, prevent, or recommend products.

03
Product and service evaluation

A practical check before relying on a claim.

The Hub publishes tools and source links, not “best product” rankings. Products and research interventions must be evaluated separately.

01

Identify the object

Is it a food, consumer product, wellness device, medical device, research device, service, or component? Record the declared intended use and jurisdiction.

02

Check the record

Use the relevant regulator database, standard issuer, or laboratory scope. A registration or listing does not automatically mean approval or validated efficacy.

03

Check the method

For dissolved hydrogen or gas output, ask for method, sampling time, temperature or container conditions, detection limit, uncertainty, and testing-lab scope.

04

Keep research separate

Do not infer that a product works because another intervention appears in a paper. Match intervention, dose, device, population, endpoint, and jurisdiction.

04
Testing Standards & Analytical Protocols

Grounded in quantitative chemistry, eliminating speculative marketing

Dissolved molecular hydrogen (H2) is a non-polar, odorless, and rapidly escaping volatile molecule. Rigorous quantification requires accredited analytical methodologies:

Universal Gold Standard

Gas Chromatography with TCD (GC-TCD)

Utilizes headspace sampling coupled with high-sensitivity Thermal Conductivity Detection (TCD) to detect partition hydrogen. The universally accepted analytical benchmark with zero interference from dissolved minerals.

  • Precision: Down to 0.01 ppm (10 ppb)
  • Use Case: Third-party certification & arbitration
  • Constraint: Requires specialized chromatography equipment
Portable Quantitative Method

Methylene Blue Colloidal Platinum Titration

A redox reagent titration where colloidal platinum catalytically reduces methylene blue in the presence of dissolved H2. Each calibrated drop consumed corresponds to approximately 0.1 ppm (100 ppb) dissolved H2.

  • Precision: Semi-quantitative (±10% accuracy)
  • Use Case: Field testing, factory acceptance audits
  • Protocol: Must use airtight amber vials with zero headspace
Analytical Caution

Oxidation-Reduction Potential (ORP) is NOT Concentration

A negative ORP (e.g. -300mV to -600mV) indicates a reducing environment but is easily triggered by dissolved minerals, vitamin C, or sulfides. IHSA standards explicitly prohibit converting ORP mV to hydrogen ppm.

  • IHSA Rule: ORP cannot serve as sole proof of H2
  • Requirement: Must be paired with selective H2 electrode or GC

Global Regulatory Frameworks & Compliance

Clarifying legal distinctions between food/beverages, water devices, and medical claims

Regulatory pathways vary significantly across jurisdictions:

China (NHC / SAMR)

Packaged hydrogen water falls under standard bottled drinking water regulations. Materials touching drinking water require provincial Health Commission safety licenses (涉水批件) meeting GB 5749. High-concentration inhalation devices require Class II/III medical device registration before health claims can be made.

United States (US FDA / FTC)

Molecular hydrogen gas is recognized as GRAS (Generally Recognized as Safe, GRN No. 520) for food and beverage infusion. FTC strictly monitors advertising claims; marketing hydrogen devices as cures for Alzheimer's, cancer, or metabolic diseases without FDA approval triggers regulatory warning letters.

Japan (MHLW / CAA)

Molecular hydrogen is listed on the official food additives registry (Additive No. 168: 水素) by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare. Inhalation therapy has been explored under Japan's Advanced Medical Care B framework, but consumer goods remain strictly within wellness boundaries.
