Solid-State Hydrogen vs PEM: Procurement Decision Tree

September 24, 2026 · By: Martin · Buyer Guide

Bottom Line: Use a procurement decision tree, not a technology slogan. Route SKUs by power availability, half-life need, and revenue model: solid-state ICR media for portable/consumable/wet-room formats; PEM for powered countertop and inhalation devices; both when the portfolio spans home and on-the-go. Lock suppliers with an RFP that demands ppb, half-life, COA, and sample lead time — not marketing decks.

1. Why procurement needs a tree, not a binary war

Answer-first: Solid-state hydrogen media and SPE/PEM electrolysis are not competitors in every RFQ — they solve different constraint sets. Media optimize for zero electricity, sustained release (18–24 h half-life), and consumable economics. PEM optimizes for instant peak concentration while powered, adjustable output, and device-category products. A procurement team that forces one winner across bottles, filters, tablets, baths, and countertop machines will mis-price warranty, certification, and refill attach rates.

This guide is the procurement layer on top of our technical Field Guide and the permanent 9-dimension comparison page. Use it when you are writing an RFP, scoring suppliers, or deciding which SKUs get media vs devices — or both.

2. Procurement decision tree (use-case → path)

  1. Is there reliable mains or battery power at the point of use?
    No → Path M (Media). Yes → continue.
  2. Is the SKU a finished device category (countertop, inhaler) with its own certification?
    Yes → Path P (PEM device). No → continue.
  3. Do you need hydrogen present hours after preparation (shelf, travel, spa session)?
    Yes → Path M. No, peak while running is enough → Path P.
  4. Do you want refill / consumable revenue (razor & blades)?
    Yes → Path M (or hybrid portfolio). No → continue.
  5. Format: bottle, cartridge, tablet, sachet, soap, foot bath, agriculture water?
    Any of these → Path M.
  6. Home appliance brand adding H₂ SKUs without electronics redesign?
    Yes → Path M embedded media. Electronics redesign acceptable → evaluate Path P BOM.
  7. Portfolio spans home + portable?
    Yes → Hybrid: PEM at home, media everywhere else.

Path M — Solid-state media (ICR)

Best when procurement must minimize electronics risk: drop-in ceramic balls, filter cartridges, tablets, and sachets. Value levers: zero power, long half-life, private-label packaging, and predictable refill cadence. Primary supplier risks: inconsistent ppb by water chemistry, short claimed life vs lab conditions, missing COA, and vague dosage guidance.

Path P — PEM / SPE device

Best for powered countertop and inhalation products where peak concentration while running matters more than residual hours later. Value levers: instant output, adjustable flow, device ASP. Primary supplier risks: membrane life, ozone/chlorine byproducts if poorly designed, battery warranty, and regional electrical certification.

Path H — Hybrid portfolio

Standard for multi-SKU brands: PEM for the flagship home machine; media for bottles, purifier drop-ins, tablets, beauty, bath, and travel. Procurement should run two RFQs in parallel with shared compliance pack (food-contact / heavy metals / claims policy) so legal review happens once.

4. Supplier evaluation checklist (score 0–2 each)

  1. Method-defined ppb — volume, contact time, temperature stated with the number.
  2. Half-life protocol — sealed storage curve, not a single peak reading.
  3. Batch COA — every lot: heavy metals, moisture, microbial as applicable.
  4. Third-party reports — SGS / regional lab on file (e.g. drinking-water hygiene where relevant).
  5. Dosage & pack guidance — g/L or pieces/L for your vessel size.
  6. Customization — diameter, release rate, blend, private label — with constraints documented.
  7. Capacity — annual tons / pieces vs your forecast; dual-site if critical.
  8. Sample SLA — working samples and paid test kits with calendar commitment.
  9. Claims discipline — supplier will not draft disease claims for your packaging.
  10. Traceability — patent / standard references (e.g. SAC/TC621 context for antimicrobial media) and change-control process.

Scoring: ≥16 shortlist; 12–15 conditional with corrective actions; <12 reject for Phase-1 sourcing. For PEM modules, swap items 2 and 6 for membrane life, byproduct testing, and electrical safety files.

5. RFP text block you can paste

RFP excerpt — solid-state hydrogen media

Supplier shall provide: (1) target dissolved H₂ ppb at [volume] mL, [contact] min, [temp] °C; (2) half-life hours under sealed storage; (3) dosage per liter and recommended pack; (4) batch COA + third-party test list; (5) MOQ, lead time, sample window; (6) OEM constraints (size, release rate, packaging); (7) written confirmation that marketing copy supplied will avoid disease treatment claims. Non-compliant bids will not be scored.

6. Decision outputs your team should document

After the tree and scoring, write a one-page sourcing note: Path M / P / H per SKU family, selected suppliers, sample results vs RFP thresholds, 3-year cost model assumptions, and compliance owner. Link that note in your PLM so engineering and marketing cannot silently reintroduce mismatched tech claims.

For category economics (refill LTV, foot-spa consumables, steamer zero-retool), continue with: Foot-spa consumable model · Steamer upgrade · Materials catalog.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

When should a brand choose solid-state hydrogen media over PEM electrolysis?

Choose solid-state media when the SKU has no reliable power path, needs 18–24 hour hydrogen half-life after preparation, targets consumable refill revenue, or must enter wet-room, travel, bath, sachet, soap, or agriculture formats. Choose PEM when you are building a countertop or inhalation device with continuous power and instant peak concentration while running.

What belongs in an RFP for solid-state hydrogen materials?

Require dissolved-hydrogen ppb at a stated water volume and contact time, half-life under sealed storage, ORP, particle size and dosage per liter, batch COA, third-party reports (SGS or equivalent), heavy metals and microbial limits, OEM customization range, MOQ and sample lead time (typical MUQI sample window 5–22 days), and stability under your product's pH and temperature.

How do total cost of ownership differ between media and PEM devices?

PEM devices concentrate cost in BOM, battery, electronics certification, and warranty; media concentrate cost in per-unit consumable and packaging. For multi-SKU portfolios that include bottles, filters, tablets, or bath products, media often produce higher lifetime gross profit through refills even when unit COGS differ. Model three-year refill attach rate before locking hardware-only economics.

Can solid-state and PEM technologies be used together in one product line?

Yes. Many brands run PEM for home countertop or inhalation products and solid-state media for portable bottles, purifier cartridges, tablets, soap, foot bath, and travel formats. The procurement decision tree in this article treats them as complementary tools: allocate by use-case, power availability, and revenue model rather than forcing a single winner.
