Solid-State Hydrogen vs PEM: Procurement Decision Tree

📅 Published: September 24, 2026 ✍️ By: Martin 🏷️ Category: Buyer Guide ⏱️ 7 min read
💡 Direct Answer

Do not pick a technology by slogan. Walk a procurement decision tree by power, half-life need, and revenue model — then score suppliers with an RFP that demands ppb, half-life, COA, and sample SLA.

💡 Key Takeaways

This is the procurement layer on top of the technical Field Guide and the 9-dimension comparison. Angle here: RFP, TCO buckets, and a 10-point supplier scorecard — not another restatement of how electrolysis works. Path M (media) for portable/consumable/wet-room SKUs; Path P (PEM) for powered devices; Path H when the portfolio spans both.

1. Why procurement needs a tree, not a binary war

Answer-first: Solid-state hydrogen media and SPE/PEM electrolysis solve different constraint sets. Media optimize for zero electricity, sustained release, and consumable economics. PEM optimizes for instant peak concentration while powered. Forcing one winner across bottles, filters, tablets, baths, and countertop machines mis-prices warranty, certification, and refill attach rates.

Figure 2 · Mechanism reference (same family as Field Guide — parameters live there; this page owns the buying workflow)

Use this page when writing an RFP, scoring suppliers, or deciding which SKUs get media vs devices — or both.

2. Procurement decision tree (use-case → path)

  1. Reliable mains or battery at point of use? No → Path M (Media). Yes → continue.
  2. Finished device category (countertop, inhaler) with its own certification? Yes → Path P. No → continue.
  3. Need hydrogen present hours after preparation? Yes → Path M. Peak-while-running enough → Path P.
  4. Want refill / consumable revenue? Yes → Path M (or hybrid).
  5. Bottle, cartridge, tablet, sachet, soap, foot bath, agriculture water? → Path M.
  6. Appliance brand adding H₂ SKUs without electronics redesign? Yes → Path M.
  7. Portfolio spans home + portable? Yes → Path H (Hybrid).

Path M — Solid-state media (ICR)

Drop-in ceramic balls, filter cartridges, tablets, and sachets. Value: zero power, long half-life, private-label packaging, predictable refill cadence. Risks to probe: ppb by water chemistry, claimed life vs lab, missing COA, vague dosage.

Figure 3 · Path M material form — ceramic media samples for RFQ testing

Path P — PEM / SPE device

Powered countertop and inhalation products where peak while running matters more than residual hours later. Risks: membrane life, byproducts if poorly designed, battery warranty, regional electrical certification.

Path H — Hybrid portfolio

PEM for the flagship home machine; media for bottles, purifier drop-ins, tablets, beauty, bath, and travel. Run two RFQs in parallel with one shared compliance pack.

Figure 4 · Hybrid portfolio formats that typically sit on Path M

According to MUQI product parameters (lab design targets, not medical claims): ICR media target 1,000–1,500 ppb dissolved hydrogen with 18–24 h half-life and near-zero operating power. Re-validate under your water matrix before locking COGS.

4. Supplier evaluation checklist (score 0–2 each)

  1. Method-defined ppb — volume, contact time, temperature stated with the number.
  2. Half-life protocol — sealed storage curve, not a single peak reading.
  3. Batch COA — every lot: heavy metals, moisture, microbial as applicable.
  4. Third-party reports — SGS / regional lab on file.
  5. Dosage & pack guidance — g/L or pieces/L for your vessel size.
  6. Customization — diameter, release rate, blend, private label — with constraints documented.
  7. Capacity — annual tons / pieces vs your forecast.
  8. Sample SLA — working samples and paid test kits with calendar commitment.
  9. Claims discipline — supplier will not draft disease claims for your packaging.
  10. Traceability — patent / standard references and change-control process.

Scoring: ≥16 shortlist; 12–15 conditional; <12 reject for Phase-1 sourcing. For PEM modules, swap items 2 and 6 for membrane life, byproduct testing, and electrical safety files.

5. RFP text block you can paste

RFP excerpt — solid-state hydrogen media

Supplier shall provide: (1) target dissolved H₂ ppb at [volume] mL, [contact] min, [temp] °C; (2) half-life hours under sealed storage; (3) dosage per liter and recommended pack; (4) batch COA + third-party test list; (5) MOQ, lead time, sample window; (6) OEM constraints; (7) written confirmation that marketing copy will avoid disease treatment claims. Non-compliant bids will not be scored.

6. Decision outputs your team should document

Write a one-page sourcing note: Path M / P / H per SKU family, selected suppliers, sample results vs RFP thresholds, 3-year cost model assumptions, and compliance owner. Link it in PLM so engineering and marketing cannot silently reintroduce mismatched tech claims.

Continue with category economics: Foot-spa consumable model · Steamer upgrade · Materials catalog.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

When should a brand choose solid-state hydrogen media over PEM electrolysis?

Choose solid-state media when the SKU has no reliable power path, needs 18–24 hour hydrogen half-life after preparation, targets consumable refill revenue, or must enter wet-room, travel, bath, sachet, soap, or agriculture formats. Choose PEM when you are building a countertop or inhalation device with continuous power and instant peak concentration while running.

What belongs in an RFP for solid-state hydrogen materials?

Require dissolved-hydrogen ppb at a stated water volume and contact time, half-life under sealed storage, ORP, particle size and dosage per liter, batch COA, third-party reports (SGS or equivalent), heavy metals and microbial limits, OEM customization range, MOQ and sample lead time (typical MUQI sample window 5–22 days), and stability under your product's pH and temperature.

How do total cost of ownership differ between media and PEM devices?

PEM devices concentrate cost in BOM, battery, electronics certification, and warranty; media concentrate cost in per-unit consumable and packaging. For multi-SKU portfolios that include bottles, filters, tablets, or bath products, media often produce higher lifetime gross profit through refills even when unit COGS differ. Model three-year refill attach rate before locking hardware-only economics.

Can solid-state and PEM technologies be used together in one product line?

Yes. Many brands run PEM for home countertop or inhalation products and solid-state media for portable bottles, purifier cartridges, tablets, soap, foot bath, and travel formats. The procurement decision tree treats them as complementary tools: allocate by use-case, power availability, and revenue model rather than forcing a single winner.
