In the European marketplace, two full-cycle experiments on hydrogen water have already been conducted on our behalf.

These are not theoretical hypotheses in academic journals, nor are they lab bench concepts. Two real commercial enterprises formulated physical products, assigned prices, placed them onto retail shelves, and sold them to consumers. Both subsequently funded university research teams to conduct clinical trials—and then either published their complete results, or chose not to.

1. Clarifying Criteria: What Constitutes a "Real-World Case"?

  • First, the product must be actively commercialized: Possessing a verifiable retail price, commercial packaging, and active sales channels.
  • Second, the corporate entity must be legally established: Registered corporate name, manufacturing origin, and legal identity.
  • Third, the clinical study must have been genuinely executed: Formal clinical registry number (NCT ID), enrolled human subjects, and institutional ethics approval.
  • Fourth, the outcomes must be publicly verifiable: Findings must either be openly accessible or demonstrably unpublished—because withholding data is itself informative.

2. Case A: The £2.49 Canned Hydrogen Water from the UK

HtoEAU is a canned hydrogen water brand marketed with UK pricing, formulated from Icelandic glacial water infused with 5 mg/L (5 ppm) dissolved molecular hydrogen at pH 8.3, retailing from £2.49 per can.

Commercial HtoEAU canned hydrogen water (£2.49/can, labeled 5 mg/L).

The study was led by Prof. Sergej Ostojić of the University of Novi Sad. The ClinicalTrials.gov record (NCT06788015) notes 10 enrolled collegiate athletes (12 claimed by brand website) in a 4-week crossover design.

Figure 2: The four physiological endpoints claimed on the brand website.

The Transparency Gap: As of October 2026, NCT06788015 shows zero posted results. PubMed contains zero peer-reviewed papers matching this study. Without peer review or public data, the brand's storefront claims cannot be independently verified.

3. Case B: The Czech National Finswimming Cohort

Nutristamina s.r.o. produces Aquastamina-R, packaged in 420 mL multilayer pouches with 0.9 ppm dissolved H₂. Clinical testing was executed by Prof. Michal Botek at Palacký University Olomouc.

Commercial Aquastamina-R hydrogen water pouch (0.9 ppm, 420 mL).

In a randomized, double-blind, crossover trial on 12 national finswimmers (NCT05799911), 400m swimming performance improved by +0.6% (P=0.003), crossing the Smallest Worthwhile Change (SWC = 0.4%). However, 50m sprint, blood lactate, RPE, and oxidative stress markers showed ZERO significant differences (4 null endpoints).

5. What These Two Cases Teach Us

1. Concentration ≠ Everything: Case B used water with 0.9 ppm dissolved H₂ (vs Case A's 5 mg/L) and still detected a statistically significant signal. Delivery protocol, timing, and athlete physiology matter far more than isolated marketing numbers.

2. Maturity = Publishing Nulls: A scientific field proves its maturity not when every study claims success, but when researchers openly publish null outcomes and methodological boundaries. The Czech team found no difference across 4 out of 5 endpoints and published anyway.

3. Verifiability is Essential: True evidence requires a pre-registered trial ID, independent university sponsorship, transparent sample size, and open data.
