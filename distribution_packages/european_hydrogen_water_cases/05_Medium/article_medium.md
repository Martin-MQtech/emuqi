# Does Hydrogen Water Work? Two Real-World European Market Cases and Their Full Dossiers

### European Molecular Hydrogen Clinical Research Cross-Investigation (UK brand HtoEAU vs Czech Aquastamina-R)

> *October 18, 2026 · By Martin Chen, CEO • MQ TECH · Category: Evidence-Based Molecular Hydrogen*

![Does Hydrogen Water Work? Two Real-World European Market Cases and Their Full Dossiers](https://www.emuqi.com/assets/images/blog/european-hydrogen-water-cases/fig5-cover-en.png)

In the European marketplace, two full-cycle experiments on hydrogen water have already been conducted on our behalf.

These are not theoretical hypotheses in academic journals, nor are they lab bench concepts.

Two real commercial enterprises formulated physical products, assigned prices, placed them onto retail shelves, and sold them to consumers. Both subsequently funded university research teams to conduct clinical trials—and then either published their complete results, or chose not to.

One brand is based in the United Kingdom, priced at £2.49 per can, with its homepage prominently displaying *"Clinically tested. Measurably effective."*

The other is based in the Czech Republic, tested on national-level finswimmers in a double-blind crossover trial, concluding that 400-meter swimming times changed by **+0.6%**.

The clinical designs of the two trials were virtually identical: randomized, double-blind, crossover, and placebo-controlled.

Their reported conclusions diverge substantially.

**Their attitudes toward scientific transparency diverge even further.**

We extracted the complete dossiers for both cases—including corporate entities, product formulations, clinical registry IDs, peer-reviewed papers, and raw open datasets. Below is the side-by-side comparative analysis of these two European journeys.

### 1. Clarifying Criteria: What Constitutes a "Real-World Case"?

In this investigation, a "real-world case" must satisfy four non-negotiable criteria:

1. **First, the product must be actively commercialized.**  
   It must possess a verifiable retail price, commercial packaging, and active sales channels—not a concept or an internal lab prototype.
2. **Second, the corporate entity must be legally established.**  
   It must have a registered corporate name, manufacturing origin, and legal identity—not vague assertions such as "developed by a specialist research team."
3. **Third, the clinical study must have been genuinely executed.**  
   It must carry a clinical registry number (e.g., ClinicalTrials.gov NCT ID), enrolled human subjects, and formal institutional ethics committee approval.
4. **Fourth, the outcomes must be publicly verifiable.**  
   The findings must either be openly accessible or demonstrably unpublished—**because withholding data is itself informative.**

Only when all four criteria are met does an investigation qualify as a case study. Without them, it remains mere marketing narrative.

**Why do real-world cases carry greater weight than selective literature reviews?**

Because literature can be cited selectively; real-world cases cannot.

In an academic review, one can easily cite a single positive finding while omitting null endpoints. But when a physical product sits on a store shelf, capital has been deployed, human subjects have completed protocols, and dataset matrices have been locked—**the resulting reality must be confronted.**

### 2. Case A: The £2.49 Canned Hydrogen Water from the UK

#### The Enterprise and Product
- **Corporate Entity**: HtoEAU LTD, registered in England & Wales (Company No. 15264660), incorporated November 2023. Registered address: 128 City Road, London, EC1V 2NX.
- **Physical Product**: Canned hydrogen water, 250 mL aluminium can, marketed at £2.49 per can.
- **Claimed Dissolved Concentration**: 5 mg/L (5 ppm).
- **Official Commercial Slogan**: *"Clinically tested. Measurably effective."*

![Figure 2: Four endpoints claimed by UK brand HtoEAU](https://www.emuqi.com/assets/images/blog/european-hydrogen-water-cases/fig2-four-endpoints-grid-en.png)

#### The Clinical Trial Claims
The brand’s official website explicitly lists four key trial outcomes:
1. Max sprint velocity increased by **+3.4%**;
2. Anaerobic threshold delayed by **+4.1%**;
3. Mental health score improved by **+11.8%**;
4. Quality of life (SF-36 physical component) increased by **+6.2%**.

The study was led by Prof. Sergej M. Ostojić, University of Novi Sad, a well-known researcher in molecular hydrogen sports physiology. The study was formally registered on ClinicalTrials.gov under identifier **NCT06788015** (*"Effects of Hydrogen-Rich Water in Healthy Active Adults"*).

#### The Evidentiary Gap
As of October 2026, a meticulous audit reveals:
- **ClinicalTrials.gov Status**: NCT06788015 remains listed as *"Completed"*, but **Zero Results Have Been Submitted**.
- **Peer-Reviewed Journal Index**: Comprehensive searches across PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science reveal **no published journal paper** matching this trial protocol.
- **Raw Data Availability**: No open repository (Zenodo, Figshare, OSF) contains the raw dataset.

**The Commercial Paradox**:  
A retail consumer buying a £2.49 can based on the homepage claim *"Clinically tested. Measurably effective"* cannot independently verify whether the trial yielded positive results, statistically neutral results, or unblinded anomalies. The consumer must rely entirely on trust.

### 3. Case B: The Czech Republic Finswimmer Protocol

#### The Enterprise and Product
- **Corporate Entity**: Aquastamina-R, developed in the Czech Republic.
- **Physical Product**: Flexible aluminium barrier pouch, 420 mL.
- **Dissolved Concentration**: 0.9 ppm (0.9 mg/L), produced via physical dissolution into deep-well spring water.

![Figure 1: Side-by-side core parameters of Case A and Case B](https://www.emuqi.com/assets/images/blog/european-hydrogen-water-cases/fig1-case-comparison-table-en.png)

#### The Clinical Study Design
- **Lead Investigators**: Michal Botek et al., Faculty of Physical Culture, Palacký University Olomouc.
- **Target Population**: Twelve national-level competitive finswimmers ($n = 12$, 6 males, 6 females; age $17.5 \pm 2.4$ years).
- **Design Methodology**: Randomized, double-blind, crossover, placebo-controlled trial.
- **Intervention Protocol**: 420 mL hydrogen-rich water (or degassed placebo) ingested 30 minutes prior to race tests.

![Figure 3: Statistically significant +0.6% improvement alongside trivial effect size](https://www.emuqi.com/assets/images/blog/european-hydrogen-water-cases/fig3-0-6-percent-axis-en.png)

#### What the Data Actually Showed
The Czech research team published their complete trial findings in the *International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance (IJSPP)*, indexed in PubMed (PMID: 35086074):

1. **400m Surface Finswimming Performance**:
   - Placebo: $232.0 \pm 14.1$ s vs HRW: $230.7 \pm 13.9$ s.
   - Time improvement: **-1.3 seconds (-0.6%, $P = 0.003$)**.
   - Smallest Worthwhile Change (SWC = 0.4%): The +0.6% improvement surpassed the SWC threshold.
   - Individual variation: 8 of 12 athletes responded positively; 4 athletes showed zero response or slight regression.
2. **50m Apnea Sprint**: No statistically significant difference ($P > 0.05$).
3. **Blood Lactate ($La^-$)**: No statistically significant difference post-race ($P > 0.05$).
4. **Rating of Perceived Exertion (RPE)**: No difference ($P > 0.05$).
5. **Oxidative Stress Biomarkers**: No difference ($P > 0.05$).

Furthermore, the research team uploaded the complete anonymized dataset to the open science repository **Zenodo** (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.5801487), accessible to any researcher worldwide.

### 5. Three Critical Lessons for Global Brands & Product Developers

#### Lesson 1: Concentration Is Not Everything
Case A claimed 5 mg/L (over 3 times atmospheric saturation), yet produced zero verifiable open papers. Case B used a standard 0.9 ppm concentration (comfortably within the open-air equilibrium limit of 1,200–1,500 ppb) and clearly documented a +0.6% performance signal. Formulation stability, water contact retention half-life, and biological timing matter far more than theoretical peak concentration figures.

#### Lesson 2: Publishing Null Results Is the True Hallmark of Scientific Maturity
In Case B, 4 out of 5 measured endpoints showed zero improvement. The Czech team did not bury the trial or retroactively change hypotheses. They published the null endpoints alongside the positive 400m data. In sports science, showing where a technology does *not* work is just as valuable as demonstrating where it does.

#### Lesson 3: Consumers and Enterprise Buyers Must Demand Verification
Whenever encountering marketing claims such as *"clinically proven"*, every professional buyer should ask three simple verification questions:
1. *What is the clinical trial registry identifier (NCT ID)?*
2. *In which peer-reviewed journal was the study published?*
3. *Where is the raw dataset archived?*
