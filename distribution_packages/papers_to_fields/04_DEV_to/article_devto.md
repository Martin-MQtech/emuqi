# From Papers to Fields: What 1,500 Papers Mean for Hydrogen Agriculture

*By Martin Chen · November 3, 2026 · Originally published on [emuqi.com](https://www.emuqi.com/blog/hydrogen-agriculture-papers-to-fields-en.html)*

**Executive Summary:** Hydrogen agriculture is not a new track — it is a spillover of hydrogen medicine and biology. More than 1,500 papers worldwide (roughly 90% from China) still add up to zero independent statistical calibers: the bottleneck is delivery, not evidence. On 29 October 2026, Martin Chen (CEO, MUQI Tech) and Professor Pei Zhou's team at the SJTU Hydrogen Science Center agreed on route alignment, evidence boundaries, and a joint field-validation plan. Data as of October 2026.

![SJTU Hydrogen Science Center discussion](https://www.emuqi.com/assets/images/blog/papers-to-fields/photo-discussion.jpg)

## 1. A spillover, not a new track

Around 2007 the “hydrogen–cell” evidence chain was established (selective antioxidation, anti-inflammation, signal regulation): China contributed 1,143 papers (22.3% of the global total) and 39 WHO ICTRP-registered trials. 2011: hydrogen becomes the fourth gas-signalling molecule and the question moves to plants — stress tolerance, heavy-metal immobilisation, saline-alkali adaptation, post-harvest preservation. From 2019: into the field, 1,500+ papers, ~90% from China (Science & Technology Review 2024 No. 15; Wang et al. 2020, J Zhejiang Univ Sci B).

![Conduction timeline](https://www.emuqi.com/assets/images/blog/papers-to-fields/chart-timeline-en.jpg)

## 2. The delivery gap

Papers prove an effect, not a deliverable. Each conversion stage loses something:

- **paper → product**: lab concentration/time does not map to operable field parameters;
- **product → commodity**: no controls, no replication, no cost ledger;
- **commodity → industry**: no standards, no testing, no insurance or subsidies.

1,500+ papers vs **zero independent statistical calibers**. The missing tool is a common ruler: what to measure, how, at what dosage.

![Delivery gap](https://www.emuqi.com/assets/images/blog/papers-to-fields/chart-gap-en.jpg)

## 3. Three global routes — scenario decides

- **Devices (JP/AU/KR)**: on-site electrolysis + brand agriculture (SUISO JAPAN's “Suilive SA-1”, Fukuoka 2025; HERO Irrigation unit at US$16,311 with an explicit no-yield-promise, blank-control-required policy). Costs: power, capex, concentration decay, maintenance.
- **Energy (EU/US/AU)**: fertiliser, machinery-fuel and cold-chain decarbonisation (e.g. NSW's A$45.2M hydrogen hub). That is input decarbonisation — not agronomy. Australia is the cleanest example of the two “hydrogen agricultures” being conflated.
- **Materials (China)**: 90% of the world's papers run on a solid-material route straight to the open field — unpowered, dispersed, low-cost scenarios.

![Three routes](https://www.emuqi.com/assets/images/blog/papers-to-fields/chart-routes-en.jpg)

## 4. The smile curve

Profit is not in device assembly. It sits at the two ends: **materials/formulation** (high margin, replicable, repurchasable) and **scenario/brand** (high premium, slow, deep moat). Six things must be built separately between paper and field: metrology & standards, product formulation, field reproducibility, cost ledgers, channels & service, testing & insurance.

![Smile curve](https://www.emuqi.com/assets/images/blog/papers-to-fields/chart-smile-curve-en.jpg)

## 5-6. The SJTU session: three consensuses, six questions

On 29 October 2026 the SJTU Hydrogen Science Center team and MUQI agreed on: **route alignment**, **evidence boundaries** (papers vs demonstrations vs controlled trials), and **next steps** (field-validation plan, samples, joint research topic). The team's six questions all target delivery:

- **01 Gas composition and purity? Any CO₂?** — Reaction yields mainly H₂, no sodium bicarbonate; full GC analysis pending. 
- **02 What are the release products? Effects on soil pH/salinity?** — SiO₂ / silicic acid / Mg–Ca products; no formal environmental safety assessment yet. Pot/field trials + ecotoxicology required.
- **03 How does the silicon fraction change before and after release?** — Si⁰ → SiO₂ / silicic acid; nano-silicon 10–50%. Total/reactive/available silicon to be separated.
- **04 How do white control granules differ from black ones?** — White is an alkaline mineral matrix; control validity depends on matching baseline chemistry. 
- **05 Will hydrogel encapsulation release hydrogen early?** — Expected on thermodynamic and kinetic grounds. Sodium-alginate coating + dry storage recommended.
- **06 Two batches, different particle size — why does release differ?** — Available-silicon content matches; difference comes from porosity, density, surface oxidation. Batch characterisation needed.

![Six questions](https://www.emuqi.com/assets/images/blog/papers-to-fields/chart-six-questions-en.jpg)

Safety note: the silicon-hydrogen reaction yields mainly H₂ with SiO₂/silicic acid and Mg–Ca products; no formal environmental safety assessment exists yet — pot trials, field trials and ecotoxicology are required before claims.

## 7. Four gates, six big pieces

Landing requires **technology → productisation → commercialisation → industrialisation**. Most of the field is stuck between gates one and two. The six “big pieces”: metrology & standards; formulation; field reproducibility; cost ledger; channels & service; testing & insurance.
