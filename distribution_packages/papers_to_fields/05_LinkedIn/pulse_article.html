From Papers to Fields: What 1,500 Papers Mean for Hydrogen Agriculture

By Martin Chen · Partner & CEO, Shandong MUQI Health Technology Co., Ltd. (MUQI Tech) · November 2026 · 8 min read

Executive summary. Hydrogen agriculture is not a new track — it is a spillover of hydrogen medicine and biology. More than 1,500 papers worldwide (roughly 90% from China) still add up to zero independent statistical calibers: the bottleneck is delivery, not evidence. On 29 October 2026, Professor Pei Zhou's team at the SJTU Hydrogen Science Center and MUQI agreed on route alignment, evidence boundaries, and a joint field-validation plan. Data as of October 2026.

1. A spillover, not a new track

Around 2007, the "hydrogen–cell" evidence chain was established — selective antioxidation, anti-inflammation, signal regulation. China contributed 1,143 papers (22.3% of the global total) and 39 WHO ICTRP-registered trials. In 2011 hydrogen was recognised as the fourth gas-signalling molecule, and the question moved to "hydrogen–plants": stress tolerance, heavy-metal immobilisation, saline-alkali adaptation, post-harvest preservation. From 2019 the work moved from mechanism toward the field: more than 1,500 papers to date, about 90% from China (per Science & Technology Review 2024 No. 15 and the review by Wang YQ et al., J Zhejiang Univ Sci B, 2020).

The hydrogen wellness industry walked this path first. Industrialisation capability — materials, products, channels, regulation — is now spilling over into agriculture: 180+ companies, a solid-material route, four institutional gates. So this is not cross-industry adventurism; it is capability reuse. The next question is no longer "does hydrogen work?" but "how do we industrialise it?"

2. Why 1,500 papers ≠ one reproducible case

Because papers prove an effect, not a deliverable. Each conversion stage loses something:

  • Paper → product: laboratory concentrations and durations do not map to operable field parameters;
  • Product → commodity: no controls, no replication, no cost ledger;
  • Commodity → industry: no standards, no testing, no insurance or subsidies.

Papers prove "effective". Zero independent statistical calibers means "not delivered". What the industry lacks most is a common ruler — what to measure, how, and at what dosage. Without it, a yield number from field A does not transfer to field B.

3. Three global routes — scenario decides the route

The device route. Japan sells hydrogen water as "equipment + brand agriculture": in summer 2025 SUISO JAPAN launched "Suilive SA-1", Japan's first agricultural hydrogen-dissolving unit, in Fukuoka (per SUISO JAPAN / Nihon Trim public information, 2025-09). The costs: power demand, expensive hardware, concentration decay, heavy maintenance.

Australia is the most instructive — one country, two entirely different "hydrogen agricultures". On one side, devices: HERO Irrigation / YBG Global lists a single unit at US$16,311 (PEM/SPE electrolysis with nanobubbles), explicitly promising no yield gains and requiring blank controls (accessed 2025-10). On the other, energy hydrogen: the NSW Government committed A$45.2 million to an end-to-end hydrogen hub replacing fossil-based fertiliser and farm fuel (2025-03). The latter is hydrogen-energy decarbonisation, not hydrogen-water agronomy — the two are constantly conflated, and Australia's lesson is precisely that.

Korea convenes; Europe and the US decarbonise. Korea's policy-driven Hydrogen Agriculture Technology Association pushes "hydrogen agriculture + smart greenhouses" with few public trial data; Europe and the US bring hydrogen into agri-food systems via fertiliser, machinery and cold chain.

China is different: 90% of the papers run on a unique material route — skipping "devices first" and taking materials straight to the open field. There is no best technology, only scenario fit: powered, piped, high-value protected agriculture can justify devices; unpowered, dispersed, low-cost saline-alkali fields call for solid materials.

4. The smile curve: where profit actually sits

Between paper and field stand six things that must be built separately: metrology and standards; product formulation; field reproducibility; cost ledgers; channels and service; testing and insurance. The deeper logic across all three routes: profit is not in device assembly but at the materials and scenario ends. Devices mean thin margins, price wars and one-off sales. Materials and formulation mean high margin, replicability, repurchase. Scenario and brand mean high premium — slow to build, but deeply moated.

5. Inside the SJTU session: three consensuses, six questions

On 29 October 2026, in Agri-Innovation Building B501 at SJTU Minhang Campus, Professor Pei Zhou's team and MUQI exchanged views point by point on the silicon-hydrogen granules. Conclusions belong to field data; three things were agreed that day:

  • Route alignment — how the three global routes differ from the Chinese approach;
  • Evidence boundaries — papers, demonstrations and controlled trials stated separately;
  • Next steps — a field-validation plan, samples and a joint research topic.

The team's six questions all targeted delivery — the standard due-diligence before anything enters a field:

  • Gas composition and purity — any CO₂? Reaction yields mainly H₂, no sodium bicarbonate; full GC analysis pending.
  • Release products, and effects on soil pH/salinity? SiO₂ / silicic acid / Mg–Ca products; no formal environmental safety assessment yet — pot and field trials plus ecotoxicology required.
  • Silicon fraction changes before and after release? Si⁰ → SiO₂ / silicic acid; nano-silicon 10–50%; total, reactive and available silicon still to be separated.
  • White control granules vs black ones? White is an alkaline mineral matrix; control validity depends on matching baseline chemistry.
  • Will hydrogel encapsulation release hydrogen early? Expected on thermodynamic and kinetic grounds; sodium-alginate coating plus dry storage recommended.
  • Two batches, different particle size — why does release differ? Available-silicon content matches; the difference comes from porosity, density and surface oxidation — batch characterisation needed.

Safety note: the silicon-hydrogen reaction yields mainly H₂ with SiO₂/silicic acid and Mg–Ca products. No formal environmental safety assessment exists yet; safety conclusions await completed testing, and we make no claims ahead of the data.

6. Four gates, six big pieces — and one open invitation

Hydrogen agriculture lands only after four gates: technology → productisation → commercialisation → industrialisation. Today most of the industry is stuck between the first and second. Technology can be proven; the four gates of industrial delivery must be built by the industry together — research teams, agri-input channels, growers, capital, standards bodies. That is what MUQI has been doing for the past year, and what we hope to complete with Shanghai Jiao Tong University. From papers to fields, we deliver together.
