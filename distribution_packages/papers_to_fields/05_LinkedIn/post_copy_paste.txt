Hydrogen agriculture is not a new track — it is a spillover.

1,500+ papers worldwide, roughly 90% from China — yet zero independently accounted, reproducible cases. The bottleneck is delivery, not evidence.

On 29 October, our team spent an afternoon with Professor Pei Zhou's team at the SJTU Hydrogen Science Center (Agri-Innovation Building B501). Three things agreed: route alignment, evidence boundaries, and a joint field-validation plan.

Three observations worth your time:

1. Papers prove an effect; industry needs reproducibility. Between paper and field sit six things that must be built separately: metrology & standards, product formulation, field reproducibility, cost ledgers, channels & service, testing & insurance.

2. Scenario decides the route. Devices fit powered, piped, high-value protected agriculture (HERO's US$16,311 unit promises no yield gains and requires blank controls). Energy hydrogen decarbonises fertiliser and machinery — that is input decarbonisation, not agronomy. China's 90% of papers run on a material route: solid materials straight to unpowered, dispersed, low-cost fields.

3. Profit sits at both ends of the smile curve — materials/formulation and scenario/brand — not in device assembly. That is why MUQI does not build machines: we turn hydrogen from a hazardous gas into a transportable, blendable, repurchasable solid material (37 patents, 15 invention patents; lab co-built with the Technical Institute of Physics and Chemistry, CAS).
