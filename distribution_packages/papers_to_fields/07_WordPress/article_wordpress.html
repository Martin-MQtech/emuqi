1. Is Hydrogen Agriculture a New Track? It Is a Spillover

Not a new industry. Around 2007 the “hydrogen–cell” evidence chain was established (selective antioxidation, anti-inflammation, signal regulation): China contributed 1,143 papers — 22.3% of the global total — and 39 WHO ICTRP-registered trials. In 2011 hydrogen was recognised as the fourth gas-signalling molecule and the question moved to “hydrogen–plants” (stress tolerance, heavy-metal immobilisation, saline-alkali adaptation, post-harvest preservation). From 2019 the work moved toward the field: 1,500+ papers, about 90% from China (per Science & Technology Review 2024 No. 15 and Wang YQ et al., J Zhejiang Univ Sci B, 2020).

The hydrogen wellness industry walked this path first: industrialisation capability in materials, products, channels and regulation is now spilling over into agriculture — 180+ companies, a solid-material route, four institutional gates. The next question is no longer “does hydrogen work?” but “how do we industrialise it?”

2. Why Can't 1,500 Papers Produce One Reproducible Case?

Because the bottleneck is delivery, not evidence. Each conversion stage loses something: paper → product (lab concentrations do not map to operable field parameters); product → commodity (no controls, no replication, no cost ledger); commodity → industry (no standards, no testing, no insurance or subsidies). Papers prove “effective”; zero independent statistical calibers means “not delivered”. What the industry lacks is a common ruler — what to measure, how, and at what dosage.

3. Three Global Routes — Scenario Decides the Route

The device route: Japan sells hydrogen water as “equipment + brand agriculture” — in summer 2025 SUISO JAPAN launched “Suilive SA-1” in Fukuoka (per SUISO JAPAN / Nihon Trim public information, 2025-09), at the cost of power demand, expensive hardware, concentration decay and heavy maintenance.

Australia is the most instructive — one country, two different “hydrogen agricultures”. Devices: HERO Irrigation / YBG Global lists a single unit at US$16,311, explicitly promising no yield gains and requiring blank controls (2025-10). Energy: NSW Government committed A$45.2 million to an end-to-end hydrogen hub replacing fossil-based fertiliser and farm fuel (2025-03). The latter is hydrogen-energy decarbonisation, not hydrogen-water agronomy — the two are constantly conflated.

Korea convenes, EU/US decarbonise. Korea's Hydrogen Agriculture Technology Association (h2zeta.or.kr) pushes “hydrogen agriculture + smart greenhouses” with few public data; Europe and the US bring hydrogen into agri-food via fertiliser, machinery, cold chain. China is different: 90% of the papers run on a unique material route — materials straight to the open field. No best technology, only scenario fit.

4. Profit Sits at Both Ends — and MUQI's Choice

Between paper and field stand six things that must be built separately: metrology and standards, product formulation, field reproducibility, cost ledgers, channels and service, testing and insurance. The deeper logic: profit is not in device assembly but at the materials and scenario ends — devices mean thin margins and price wars; materials/formulation mean high margin, replicability, repurchase; scenario/brand means high premium and deep moats. MUQI's choice is explicit: no complete machines — materials and formulations, turning hydrogen from a hazardous gas into a transportable, blendable, repurchasable solid material. MUQI's laboratory was co-built with the Technical Institute of Physics and Chemistry, CAS; founded 2014, 12 years of industrialisation, 37 patents (15 invention patents), 800 m² lab, four product lines (soil / aquaculture / drinking water / health). Company demonstration basis: ¥0.8 per m², 30–60 min action time, 3–6 month cycle, zero electricity (demonstration data, not a controlled trial; no yield promise).

5. What Was Actually Agreed in Shanghai — Three Consensus Items

Conclusions belong to field data. The three things agreed on 29 October 2026: route alignment (how the three global routes differ from the Chinese approach); evidence boundaries (papers, demonstrations and controlled trials stated separately); next steps (a field-validation plan, samples and a joint research topic).

7. What Comes Next: Four Gates and Six Big Pieces

Only after four gates does hydrogen agriculture land: technology → productisation → commercialisation → industrialisation. Most of the industry is still between gates one and two. Before landing, six “big pieces” remain: metrology and standards; product formulation; field reproducibility; cost ledger; channels and service; testing and insurance. Technology can be proven — the four gates of industrial delivery must be built by the industry together. From papers to fields, we deliver together.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is hydrogen agriculture — and is it the same thing as hydrogen energy?
No. Hydrogen agriculture refers to hydrogen or hydrogen-bearing materials acting on crops and water, originating from the three-stage spillover of hydrogen medicine into biology and then agriculture. Hydrogen energy addresses decarbonisation of fertiliser, machinery fuel and cold chains — different customers, price points and validation methods.

Is there real scientific evidence behind hydrogen agriculture?
Yes — 1,500+ papers worldwide, roughly 90% from China. Hydrogen has been the fourth gas-signalling molecule since 2011, with mechanistic work on stress tolerance, heavy-metal immobilisation, saline-alkali adaptation and post-harvest preservation.

If there are so many papers, why no large-scale adoption yet?
Because papers prove an effect, not a deliverable. Between paper and industry sit six things that must be built separately: metrology and standards, product formulation, field reproducibility, cost ledgers, channels and service, testing plus insurance.

How do I choose between the device route and the material route?
Choose by scenario, not by technology. Powered, piped, high-value protected agriculture can justify electrolysis devices; unpowered, dispersed, low-cost saline-alkali fields and open farmland call for solid materials.

Are solid-state hydrogen materials safe for agricultural use?
The silicon-hydrogen materials discussed here react mainly to release hydrogen, yielding SiO₂ / silicic acid and Mg–Ca products. No formal environmental safety assessment exists yet: pot and field trials plus ecotoxicology are required — listed as a priority topic for joint validation with SJTU. Safety conclusions await completed testing.
